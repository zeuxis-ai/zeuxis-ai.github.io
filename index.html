The brief already contains the building.

A developer wants a return. The city regulates the geometry. The environment sets the targets. Six hundred pages of brief, zoning and landscape law, and thousands of hours spent composing one volume out of everything it has to be.

What it does

  1. Reads the documents. The brief, the zoning code, the preservation constraints, the noise and cost limits. Not a parameter form. The constraints already exist, written down.
  2. Generates every volume that satisfies them. Real typologies with real roofs, at the dimensions the rules and the plan actually permit. Exhaustively, because that is the only way to know nothing viable was missed.
  3. Simulates each one properly. Energy, daylight, cost, on watertight geometry through a real solver. Not a proxy score.
  4. Throws almost all of it away. What arrives is a handful, and the evidence of what it survived. Every discarded scheme is inspectable, with the reason it was cut.

Position

We do not design the building. We find the few worth looking at, so the architect starts there on day one instead of month three.

An architect does not want a bigger search space. They want a smaller one. Generation is the mechanism. Reduction is the product.

The name

Zeuxis was commissioned by the city of Croton to paint Helen, the most beautiful woman in the world. He could not find a single model who had all of it, so he chose five and composed one image from the best of each. Note what the story actually says: Helen already existed, distributed across the women of Croton. The work was the looking.
